Module 1 · Foundations of Health Assessment

Cumulative content (Exams 1–2)
3–4 Qs

Written guide Readings: ATI Health Assess 3.0: Intro to Health Assessment · ATI RN Fundamentals Ch 45 Sensory Perception (nursing interventions)

7 on both · 2 written guide · 1 lecture

PurposeBoth
PPT: establish a baseline, identify concerns, guide care. Guide: purpose and definition. ATI definition: the collection, clinical judgment, and evaluation of data to plan and deliver patient-centered care. Six purposes: baseline, database, decisions, plan care, evaluate care, promote health.
Nursing process & clinical reasoningWritten guide
Assessment is the first step of the nursing process. Health assessment trains the first two steps of clinical judgment: recognize cues (history and exam) and analyze cues (expected vs. unexpected).
Subjective vs. objectiveBoth
Subjective = what the client reports (symptoms, feelings, the client's perspective). The client is the primary source; family, caregivers, and records are secondary. Objective = what you observe or measure (signs, observable indicators, instruments such as a BP cuff or pulse oximeter).
Comprehensive vs. focusedBoth
Comprehensive = head-to-toe baseline (admission, annual exam, stable client). Focused = one system or concern happening now. A client gasping for air gets a focused respiratory assessment first.
InspectionBoth
Look first, always. Start with the broad general survey, then systematic close inspection using sight, hearing, and smell, plus bedside equipment.
PalpationBoth
Dorsal (back of the) hand for temperature (thinner skin). Finger pads for fine discrimination: texture, moisture, lumps, and pulses.
PercussionBoth
Tapping to judge the density underneath: resonance over lungs, tympany over the stomach and bowel, dullness over solid organs or masses.
AuscultationBoth
Earpieces angled forward toward the nose; chest piece directly on skin. Diaphragm = high-pitched (breath sounds, bowel sounds, S1/S2). Bell = low-pitched (murmurs, bruits, S3/S4).
Order of techniquesLecture
Inspect → palpate → percuss → auscultate. Exception: the abdomen is inspect → auscultate → percuss → palpate.
Sensory perception (ATI Ch 45)Written guide
Standard ATI interventions (not in the slides): face the client in good light, speak clearly without shouting, reduce background noise, make sure glasses and hearing aids are in and working, orient to the room, and keep the call light and paths clear.

Module 2 · General Survey & Health History

Cumulative content (Exams 1–2)
3–4 Qs

Written guide Readings: ATI Health Assess 3.0: Overview of General Survey; Health History Components · ATI RN Fundamentals Ch 26 Data Collection & General Survey, Ch 35 Cultural & Spiritual Care

7 on both · 2 written guide

General surveyBoth
Starts at first contact and never really stops. Four areas: physical appearance, body structure, mobility, behavior.
Immediate distress firstBoth
PPT: identify immediate distress before the routine assessment. Guide: the doorway look for respiratory, painful, neurological, or emotional distress. If the client looks unstable, stop and prioritize ABCs; the history can wait.
Vital signsWritten guide
Adult expected: T 36–38 °C, HR 60–100, RR 12–20, BP <120/<80, SpO₂ 95–100%. Judge the pattern and the client's baseline; recheck an unexpected value.
Objective documentationBoth
Chart what you see, not what you conclude: “wringing hands, speaking rapidly,” not “anxious.” PPT: avoid assumptions.
Safety & verificationBoth
Hand hygiene, two identifiers (full name and date of birth), privacy. AIDET: Acknowledge, Introduce, Duration, Explanation, Thank you. Guide adds: trauma-informed care and environmental safety (clear path to the exit, ask how they want to be addressed, consent before touch).
Abuse, neglect, traffickingBoth
Interview the client privately, nonjudgmental questions, follow mandatory reporting laws, never confront the suspected abuser. Red flags: injuries that don't match the story, a companion who controls the conversation, fear. Use a qualified interpreter, never a family member or partner.
History componentsBoth
Chief concern verbatim, in quotation marks (guide); HPI; past medical and surgical history (childhood illness, chronic illness, injuries, admissions, surgeries, blood products); medications; allergies with the actual reaction; ROS.
OLDCARTS & PQRSTBoth
OLDCARTS: Onset, Location, Duration, Characteristics, Aggravating/relieving, Radiation, Timing, Severity. PQRST (pain): Provocation/palliation, Quality, Region/radiation, Severity, Timing.
Review of systemsWritten guide
The ROS is subjective. Record pertinent positives and pertinent negatives (“denies chest pain”). Objective findings belong in the physical exam.
🚩 Escalate / report now: Unstable at the doorway (breathing, color, LOC, severe pain) → stop the routine and prioritize.

Module 3 · Respiratory Assessment

Cumulative content (Exams 1–2)
3–4 Qs

Written guide Readings: ATI Health Assess 3.0: Respiratory System Overview · ATI RN Fundamentals Ch 29 (thorax and pulmonary), Ch 53 Airway Maintenance

6 on both · 3 written guide

A&PBoth
Right lung 3 lobes, left 2. Alveoli = gas exchange. Guide adds: upper tract (nose, pharynx, larynx: filter, warm, humidify) vs. lower tract (trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli); diaphragm is the primary muscle, accessory muscles (SCM, scalenes, trapezius) help when breathing is hard; visceral and parietal pleura; the brainstem controls breathing.
LifespanWritten guide
Children: small airways, infants are obligate nose breathers, large tongue. Older adults: less compliance, weaker cough, higher aspiration and infection risk; illness may show only as confusion.
SubjectiveWritten guide
Shortness of breath, cough and sputum, pleuritic chest pain (sharp, worse with breathing), home oxygen or equipment, smoking (pack-years = packs/day × years).
InspectionBoth
RR, SpO₂, work of breathing, symmetry, skin color. Guide: retractions (subcostal, intercostal, suprasternal/tracheal tug, supraclavicular, substernal) and paradoxical chest wall movement.
Chest shapeBoth
Normal AP : transverse ≈ 1 : 2. Barrel chest ≈ 1 : 1 (air trapping, COPD).
PalpationWritten guide
Thumbs at the 10th rib: expansion should be symmetric.
Auscultation techniqueBoth
Diaphragm on bare skin, slow deep breaths through an open mouth, side to side in a ladder pattern across anterior, lateral, and posterior fields down to T10.
Normal breath soundsBoth
Bronchial: trachea; loud, high; expiration longer. Bronchovesicular: major bronchi; medium; equal. Vesicular: most of the lung; soft, low; inspiration longer. A normal sound in the wrong place is abnormal.
Adventitious soundsBoth
Wheezes = narrowed airways. Crackles (fine or coarse, rales) = fluid or airways popping open. Rhonchi = secretions (may clear with a cough). Stridor = upper-airway obstruction: emergency. Guide adds: pleural friction rub, expiratory grunting.
🚩 Escalate / report now: PPT: new hypoxemia, severe respiratory effort, altered mental status. Guide: SpO₂ under 92%, acute stridor, asymmetric chest expansion, worsening shortness of breath, altered LOC or acute agitation.

Module 4 · Lifespan, Skin & Lymphatic Assessment

Cumulative content (Exams 1–2)
3–4 Qs

Written guide Readings: ATI Health Assess 3.0: Skin; Breast and lymphatics; Older Adult · ATI RN Fundamentals Ch 25 Older Adults, Ch 30 Integumentary and Peripheral Vascular, Ch 55 Pressure Injury, Wounds, and Wound Management

6 on both · 1 powerpoint · 2 written guide

Assessment across the lifespanBoth
PPT: adapt to age, development, and communication needs. Guide: normal growth and development from infancy through older adulthood. Infants: quiet and gentle, fontanelles (anterior closes 12–18 months). Toddlers: play. Teens: privacy. Older adults: extra time, glasses and hearing aids.
Older adult skinPowerPoint
Fragile skin, reduced elasticity, increased injury risk.
Skin assessmentBoth
Color, warmth, moisture, turgor, integrity. In darker skin, look where pigment is lowest (sclera, conjunctiva, oral mucosa, palms, soles) and use touch: warmth, edema, induration.
Skin & lymph A&PWritten guide
Epidermis (avascular), dermis (vessels, nerves, glands), hypodermis (fat). Lymph node chains: cervical, axillary, epitrochlear, inguinal. Guide also lists: subjective history, psychosocial impact, and SDOH.
Pressure injury stagesBoth
Stage 1: intact, nonblanchable. Stage 2: partial-thickness loss. Stage 3: full thickness, fat visible. Stage 4: exposed or palpable bone, tendon, muscle. Unstageable: slough or eschar hides the depth. Deep tissue injury: persistent deep red, maroon, or purple.
Braden ScaleBoth
Lower score = higher risk. Range 6–23; ≤18 = at risk. Six subscales: sensory perception, moisture, activity, mobility, nutrition, friction and shear. A subscale of 1 needs its own action.
Medical device-related pressure injuryWritten guide
Shaped like the device. The Braden doesn't capture device risk, so check under devices (tubing, masks, splints) at least twice daily.
Tunneling vs. underminingBoth
Tunneling: a narrow channel into deeper tissue. Undermining: a wide shelf of tissue loss under the wound edge. Describe both with a clock face (12 = toward the head); measure in cm with a sterile cotton-tipped applicator.
Lymph nodesBoth
Assess size and mobility. Expected: not palpable or <1 cm, soft, mobile, nontender. Tender and mobile = infection. Hard, fixed, >1 cm, painless = concern.
🚩 Escalate / report now: Guide: central cyanosis, petechiae or purpura, rapidly spreading cellulitis, new jaundice. PPT (final prioritization slide): rapid infection progression, tissue breakdown, impaired perfusion.

Module 5 · Cardiovascular Assessment

Cumulative content (Exams 1–2)
3–4 Qs

Written guide Readings: ATI Health Assess 3.0: Cardiovascular · ATI RN Fundamentals Ch 29 (heart section), Ch 30 Integumentary and Peripheral Vascular

7 on both · 3 written guide

Blood flowBoth
Right heart → lungs; left heart → body. Body → vena cavae → right atrium → tricuspid → right ventricle → pulmonic → lungs → left atrium → mitral → left ventricle → aortic → body.
ConductionBoth
SA node → AV node → bundle of His → bundle branches → Purkinje fibers. The SA node is the pacemaker; the AV node pauses so the ventricles fill.
Heart locationWritten guide
In the mediastinum. Base near the 2nd ICS; apex and PMI at the 5th ICS, left midclavicular line.
SubjectiveBoth
PPT: chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, fatigue, edema. Guide: PQRST and the A&P link: chest pain = ischemia, orthopnea = fluid in the lungs, palpitations = rhythm, edema = venous congestion.
MI in women; chest pain in childrenWritten guide
Women often have no classic chest pain: back, neck, or jaw pain, nausea, shortness of breath, fatigue. Chest pain in children is usually not cardiac (muscle, lung, GI, anxiety), but exertional pain or fainting needs follow-up.
Perfusion & extremitiesBoth
PPT: pulses, capillary refill, temperature, edema, CMS. Pulses graded 0–4+ (2+ normal), compare sides; carotids one at a time. Cap refill <2–3 s. Edema: 1+ 0–2 mm, 2+ 3–4 mm, 3+ 5–6 mm, 4+ 8 mm. Arterial = pale, cool, hairless, weak pulses; venous = swelling, brown staining.
JVDBoth
PPT: JVD with edema or dyspnea may suggest cardiac dysfunction. Check with the HOB at 30–45°; distention = backup from the right heart (fluid overload, right-sided failure).
A-P-E-T-M landmarksBoth
Aortic 2nd ICS right sternal border · Pulmonic 2nd ICS left · Erb's point 3rd ICS left · Tricuspid 4th ICS left · Mitral 5th ICS left MCL. Diaphragm first, then bell.
S1 & S2Both
S1 (lub) = AV valves (mitral, tricuspid) close; loudest at the apex. S2 (dub) = semilunar valves (aortic, pulmonic) close; loudest at the base.
Tips for auscultation; diagnosticsWritten guide
Quiet room, bare skin, client relaxed; listen at each site long enough to hear S1 and S2 separately. Know the common tests by name (ECG, echocardiogram, cardiac enzymes, lipid panel) and terms (murmur, bruit, S3, S4).
🚩 Escalate / report now: PPT: new chest pain, syncope, poor perfusion, sudden pulse changes. Guide: abnormal findings to report and safety escalation (lecture: new murmur, S3 in an older adult, absent pulse, one warm, red, swollen calf).

Module 6 · HEENT Assessment

Cumulative content (Exams 1–2)
3–4 Qs

Written guide Readings: ATI Health Assess 3.0: Head, Neck, and Neurological · ATI RN Fundamentals Ch 28 Head and Neck

6 on both · 2 written guide

PERRLABoth
Pupils Equal, Round, Reactive to Light and Accommodation. Direct (lit eye constricts) and consensual (other eye constricts too).
EOMsBoth
Six cardinal fields of gaze in an “H”, head still, finger 12–18 inches away. Eyes move together smoothly.
Snellen & confrontationBoth
Snellen = visual acuity (20 ft, one eye at a time). Confrontation = visual fields.
Weber & RinneBoth
Weber: fork on top of the head; expected midline, equal in both ears. Rinne: expected air conduction > bone conduction.
Mouth, neck, thyroid, carotidsBoth
Mouth and swallowing, tongue midline (CN XII), uvula rises (IX, X). Trachea midline. Thyroid palpated from behind as the client swallows. Carotids one side at a time; listen for a bruit.
Link each cranial nerve to its testBoth
I smell · II acuity and fields · III, IV, VI pupils and EOMs · V facial sensation, clench jaw · VII smile, close eyes, puff cheeks · VIII hearing · IX, X “ah,” gag, swallow · XI shrug · XII tongue.
Anatomy, prep & SDOHWritten guide
Equipment: penlight, otoscope, tuning fork, Snellen chart, tongue depressor. Ask about the cost of glasses or hearing aids and other barriers.
Head, face, ears, noseWritten guide
Face symmetry; palpate the TMJ. Otoscope: canal clear, TM pearly gray and intact. Nose: patency one side at a time; palpate frontal and maxillary sinuses.
🚩 Escalate / report now: PPT: sudden vision loss, facial droop, new pupil changes with neuro symptoms. Guide/lecture adds: sudden hearing loss, worst headache with neuro changes, drooling with stridor, tracheal deviation.

Module 8 · GU & Rectal Assessment

New content since Exam 2
4–5 Qs

Written guide Readings: ATI Health Assess 3.0: Rectum and Genitourinary · ATI RN Fundamentals Ch 29 (abdomen), Ch 39 Nutrition and Oral Hydration

8 on both · 3 powerpoint · 2 written guide

Urinary anatomyBoth
Kidneys → ureters → bladder → urethra. PPT: kidneys filter waste and regulate fluid, electrolytes, and BP. Urge to void at about 300 mL. Female urethra 3–4 cm (more UTIs); male about 20 cm.
Rectal & anal anatomyWritten guide
The rectum is the end of the sigmoid colon. Internal sphincter = involuntary; external = voluntary.
SubjectiveBoth
PPT: frequency, urgency, dysuria, nocturia, incontinence. Guide: reproductive subjective assessment too.
Urinary retentionBoth
Weak stream, difficulty voiding, suprapubic fullness. Palpate above the symphysis pubis; bladder scan if indicated. Lecture: scan within 10 minutes of voiding; PVR 50–100 mL expected, >100 mL notify.
UTIBoth
Dysuria, urgency, frequency, suprapubic discomfort; cloudy or foul urine, blood. Older adults may show only new confusion.
BPHPowerPoint
Hesitancy, dribbling, nocturia, incomplete emptying (Exam 2 case: T.K., 73).
Know these termsPowerPoint
Hematuria blood in urine · oliguria low output · polyuria large output · dysuria painful urination · nocturia waking at night to void.
Urine appearanceBoth
Color, clarity, odor, amount, change from baseline. Pale straw expected; dark amber concentrated; cloudy possible infection; pink or red possible blood; brown or tea-colored escalate.
Urine output calculationBoth
PPT: output (mL/hr) = total output ÷ hours; benchmark ≥30 mL/hr; investigate low output. Exam 2 lecture: mL ÷ kg ÷ hours, ≥0.5 mL/kg/hr.
Bristol stool chartBoth
Types 1–2 constipation · 3–4 formed (ideal) · 5–7 looser (diarrhea).
Melena vs. hematocheziaPowerPoint
Melena = black, tarry stool (upper GI bleeding). Hematochezia = red blood in stool.
Health promotionBoth
STI prevention (condoms are the only contraceptive that also prevents STIs), HPV vaccine (from age 9), recommended screening. Guide adds: Pap smear (every 3 years ages 21–29) and testicular self-exam. PPT: private, respectful, individualized teaching.
Lifespan, SDOH, gender-affirming care, termsWritten guide
Children need caregiver history; older adults: incontinence is common but not normal aging. Ask about barriers and supports. Gender-affirming care: ask about current anatomy and treatments, never assume.
🚩 Escalate / report now: PPT (prioritization slide): markedly low output, painful retention, signs of systemic infection.
⚠️ Heads-up: The two output rules agree for an average adult. 0.5 mL/kg/hr × 60 kg = 30 mL/hr. If a question gives a weight, use mL/kg/hr; if it only gives total mL and hours, compare with 30 mL/hr.

Module 9 · GI & Abdominal Assessment

New content since Exam 2
4–5 Qs

Written guide Readings: ATI Health Assess 3.0: Abdomen

10 on both · 2 powerpoint · 1 written guide

Anatomy & functionBoth
Stomach = digestion · small intestine = nutrient absorption · large intestine = water absorption and stool formation · accessory organs: liver, gallbladder, pancreas.
QuadrantsBoth
RUQ liver, gallbladder · LUQ stomach, spleen · RLQ appendix, cecum · LLQ descending and sigmoid colon.
SubjectiveBoth
Pain, nausea and vomiting, appetite, bowel habits. Use PQRST; consider meals, diet, hydration, medications, surgeries.
SequenceBoth
Inspect → Auscultate → Percuss → Palpate. Auscultate before you touch, because touch changes bowel sounds.
Quadrant orderBoth
Bowel sounds starting in the RLQ → RUQ → LUQ → LLQ.
InspectionBoth
Contour, symmetry, distention, skin, umbilicus.
Bowel soundsBoth
Normoactive, hypoactive, hyperactive, absent. Confirm “absent” only after prolonged listening (lecture: about 5 minutes). High-pitched tinkling with distention = possible obstruction.
PercussionBoth
Tympany = air (most of a healthy abdomen). Dullness = solid tissue, fluid, or stool.
PalpationBoth
Light palpation for tenderness and guarding; painful area last. Guarding = tensing with pain (can be voluntary). Rigidity = involuntary, boardlike.
DistentionPowerPoint
May reflect gas, fluid, stool, or obstruction.
CVA tendernessBoth
PPT: possible renal concern. Client sitting; flat hand over the angle of the 12th rib and spine, strike it with the other fist; both sides.
Bleeding termsPowerPoint
Hematemesis = blood in vomit · melena = black, tarry stool · hematochezia = red blood in stool.
GI/abdominal terminologyWritten guide
Review the Mod 9 terminology slide: dysphagia, ascites, distention, guarding, rigidity, hematemesis, melena, hematochezia.
🚩 Escalate / report now: PPT: sudden severe pain, rigidity, persistent vomiting or distention; GI bleeding with instability needs prompt action; pulsatile abdominal mass: do not palpate, escalate.
⚠️ Heads-up: Percuss vs. palpate order. The PowerPoint and the written guide both list Inspect → Auscultate → Percuss → Palpate. The Mod 9 lecture slide put palpation before percussion. Go with the review deck and guide; either way, auscultation always comes right after inspection.

Module 10 · Neurological Assessment

New content since Exam 2
4–5 Qs

Written guide Readings: ATI Health Assess 3.0: Musculoskeletal and neurological · ATI RN Fundamentals Ch 31 Musculoskeletal and Neurologic Systems

10 on both · 2 written guide

AnatomyBoth
CNS = brain and spinal cord. PNS = cranial and spinal nerves. Cerebrum: movement, sensation, language, memory. Cerebellum: coordination and balance. Brainstem: breathing, HR, consciousness, cranial nerves. Lecture: frontal (movement, judgment), parietal (sensation), temporal (hearing, memory), occipital (vision).
SubjectiveBoth
Headache, dizziness, weakness, numbness, vision or speech changes.
Mental statusBoth
LOC, orientation ×4 (person, place, time, situation), memory, speech, following commands. Know the baseline and recognize acute change: a change in LOC is the earliest sign of decline.
Glasgow Coma ScaleBoth
Eye (1–4) + verbal (1–5) + motor (1–6) = 3–15. 15 is best; 8 or less is severe.
PupilsBoth
Size, equality, shape, light response.
Motor strengthBoth
0 none · 1 trace (flicker) · 2 moves with gravity eliminated · 3 against gravity · 4 some resistance · 5 normal. Compare sides; pronator drift = weakness.
Deep tendon reflexesBoth
0 absent · 1+ diminished · 2+ expected · 3+ brisk · 4+ hyperactive (with clonus). Biceps and brachioradialis C5–6, patellar L2–4, Achilles S1–2.
CoordinationBoth
Finger-to-nose, rapid alternating movements, Romberg (feet together, eyes closed: slight sway is negative/expected).
Cranial nervesWritten guide
Same tests as Mod 6 (see that table).
BE FAST stroke screenBoth
Balance, Eyes, Face, Arms, Speech, Time. Left-brain stroke → right-sided weakness.
Stroke prioritiesBoth
Document last known well · activate the stroke response · check blood glucose (rule out hypoglycemia) · NPO until swallow screen · time is brain. Lecture adds: HOB 30°, neuro checks every 15 minutes, stat CT, SBAR.
Lifespan, SDOH, termsWritten guide
Infants: high-pitched cry or bulging fontanelle = possible increased ICP. Pregnancy: new headache or vision change = possible preeclampsia. Older adults: sudden confusion is delirium, not aging. Terms: aphasia vs. dysarthria, dysphagia, ataxia, hemiparesis, paresthesia.
🚩 Escalate / report now: PPT: new facial droop, weakness, slurred speech, decreased LOC, unequal pupils, seizures, sudden weakness.

Module 11 · Musculoskeletal Assessment

New content since Exam 2
4–5 Qs

Written guide Readings: ATI Health Assess 3.0: Musculoskeletal and neurological · ATI RN Fundamentals Ch 31 Musculoskeletal and Neurologic Systems

5 on both · 2 powerpoint · 2 written guide · 1 lecture

Anatomy & physiologyBoth
Bones: support and protection (206; also calcium storage and blood cell production). Ligaments: bone to bone. Tendons: muscle to bone. Joints enable movement. Hinge: knee, elbow. Ball and socket: hip, shoulder (shoulder = most range, least stability). Muscles: skeletal, smooth, cardiac.
SubjectiveBoth
Pain, stiffness, swelling, weakness, injury (mechanism: fall, twist, overuse), function (walking, stairs, ADLs). Lecture: timing (morning stiffness, night pain) and red flags (fever, weight loss, numbness).
ObjectiveBoth
Inspect alignment, symmetry, swelling, deformity → palpate tenderness, warmth, crepitus → ROM active and passive → strength (0–5), gait, balance, posture. Compare both sides!
Expected vs. unexpectedBoth
Expected: symmetric movement, functional ROM, stable joints, steady gait. Unexpected: swelling, redness, warmth, deformity, limited ROM, weakness, atrophy, crepitus, instability, altered gait. Lecture examples: ulnar deviation, kyphosis, lordosis, scoliosis, hallux valgus, flattened shoulder (dislocation).
Neurovascular checkPowerPoint
Color, warmth, movement, sensation, pulses, cap refill, below the injury and compared with the other side.
DocumentationPowerPoint
Site, severity, and a bilateral comparison.
LifespanBoth
PPT: children = growth and development; older adults = bone and muscle loss, fall risk. Lecture: infants (hip dysplasia screen), children (growth plates, scoliosis), older adults (osteoporosis, sarcopenia, osteoarthritis, kyphosis).
SDOHWritten guide
Physically demanding jobs without sick leave, cost of equipment and supplements, health literacy, access to physical therapy, home hazards (stairs, lighting), isolation, food insecurity limiting calcium and vitamin D.
TerminologyWritten guide
ROM, AROM vs. PROM, strength vs. tone, atrophy vs. hypertrophy, crepitus, contracture, gait, posture, symmetry, abduction vs. adduction, pronation vs. supination, dorsiflexion vs. plantar flexion.
Lecture caseLecture
85-year-old with osteoporosis, fall onto the hip, pain 10/10, pulses 2+ and cap refill <2 s; x-ray shows hip fracture needing ORIF. Orders: NV checks every 15 minutes, ice 20 min on/off, IV LR, morphine.
🚩 Escalate / report now: PPT: loss of distal pulse, new numbness, severe pain with neurovascular changes.
⚠️ Heads-up: Two slide errors in the Mod 11 case. The client hurt her right hip, but the orders and x-ray say left (in practice: clarify a side mismatch before acting). Left-leg strength is listed as “+2,” which can't be right with full ROM; read it as 5/5.

PowerPoint Clinical reasoning: Recognize → prioritize → act → reassess.

Study tips PowerPoint

  • Know assessment purpose, sequence, landmarks, and technique.
  • Distinguish expected from unexpected findings.
  • Connect abnormal findings with possible causes.
  • Know when to reassess, intervene, or escalate.
  • Use the study guide to target weak areas.
  • Practice applying concepts to patient scenarios.
  • Ask: What is most concerning? What else do I need? What comes first?

Test-taking on this exam Lecture

  • Name the module first, then ask what's expected for this age and this client's baseline.
  • Watch the action word: first, priority, best, report. Priority answers threaten ABCs or are sudden and new.
  • SATA: judge every option true or false on its own.
  • Assessment before action: if the stem lacks data, the answer is often to assess further or compare sides.
  • Pick documentation with numbers, locations, and objective words.

Practical reminders PowerPoint

Also on the review PowerPoint. This is the skills practical, not the written final.

  • Scenario and instructor are assigned randomly on practical day.
  • Start with introductions, hand hygiene, two identifiers, and safety.
